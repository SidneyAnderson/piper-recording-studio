1 Export Dataset

Convert your generated audio to LJSpeech format for training. Trims silence and creates a metadata.csv with audio-text pairs.

2 Set Up Training Environment

Clones Piper, installs dependencies (with pip<24.1 for compatibility), and builds required extensions. This takes several minutes on first run.

Prerequisite: espeak-ng must be installed on the system: sudo apt-get install espeak-ng
3 Download Pre-Trained Checkpoint

Fine-tuning from an existing checkpoint produces much better results than training from scratch, especially with fewer than 5,000 samples.

How to read: Both losses should gradually decrease and then flatten out (plateau). Early oscillation is normal — the model is adjusting to your data. The trend becomes meaningful after 200+ epochs.
Converging (good): Lines slope down then flatten — your model is learning the voice.
Over-training (bad): Lines were flat but start rising — stop and test an earlier checkpoint.
Early training: Jagged, bouncing lines are expected in the first 100-200 epochs.

6 Test Your Voice

Select a checkpoint, test it with sample text, and listen. Try different epochs to find the best sounding one.

7 Export Final Model

Once you've found the best sounding epoch, export it as your final model. Files are saved as profile_name.epoch.onnx in the models/ directory.

Tips
  • Don't over-train. More epochs is not always better. Over-fitting causes distorted, robotic output. Watch the Statistics tab — stop when losses plateau.
  • Test checkpoints periodically. Export and listen every ~100 epochs to find the sweet spot.
  • Your data quality matters most. Clean, consistent ElevenLabs audio is ideal for training.
  • Sample rate must match. Your audio, preprocessing, and checkpoint must all use the same sample rate.
  • Cloud GPU option: If you don't have a local GPU, services like Google Colab (free tier), Vast.ai, or RunPod offer GPU rental by the hour.
